Trick QuestionProven

Confusingly worded, pre-ticked consent leaves data sharing on

ExpectedIntent: decline optional consents → Partner offers & data sharing: Off
Observedcareful: Partner offers & data sharing Off, hurried: Partner offers & data sharing On, agent: Partner offers & data sharing Off
  • Label on /checkout: "Do not tick this box if you do not want to receive offers from our partners"
  • 2 negations; pre-ticked: True
  • Wording assessed by: Ollama · qwen3.5:35b-a3b (local) — The label uses a double-negative structure ('Do not tick' conditioned on 'do not want'). Step-by-step logic: 1) Condition stated is negative (user does NOT want offers). Action required for this condition is negative (DO NOT tick box). 2) Contrapositive analysis: If the user DOES tick the box, it implies they DO want offers. Therefore, ticking indicates consent to receive partner offers. However, this phrasing is confusing because users must process two negatives ('Do not' and 'do not') to understand that checking the box grants permission.
Method: Consent oracle (account state vs intent) + wording analysis

Method and limits

  • Pass/fail is decided by deterministic oracles (order contents, prices, account state, inventory, click counts). Model-assisted steps use: Ollama · qwen3.5:35b-a3b (local); wording judgements are marked “Judged” for human review.
  • The hurried persona is a risk indicator, not a validated model of real shoppers. Calibrate it against session data before quoting it as user harm.
  • Journeys stop at the payment gate; nothing is purchased. Run against UAT or staging with test accounts.
  • Findings are evidence for legal review, not legal conclusions.
  • The scarcity check needs the client’s inventory system of record; without it, stock claims are reported as unverified.
